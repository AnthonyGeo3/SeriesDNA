About Series DNA

Series DNA turns a TV show into a barcode: one stripe per episode, coloured by its rating. Put two shows side by side and you can see at a glance where each one hit its golden era, sagged in the middle, and stuck (or crashed) the landing.

What we set out to do

Make a fun, quick way to settle the “which show is better?” argument: something you can open from a link on your phone, get in a second, and then play with. It started as a two-minute live demo for a team and grew into a site anyone can land on cold. Two rules guided it: it has to be understandable without an explanation (this page is the extra credit), and it has to keep working when the Wi-Fi doesn't.

How to read the strips

  • 8.8 and up: outstanding
  • 8.4 to 8.7: great
  • 8.0 to 8.3: good
  • 7.6 to 7.9: so-so
  • under 7.6: weak
  • no rating yet

Ticks under each strip mark the seasons. A solid outline marks the Golden Era (the best run of five episodes) and a dashed one marks the Drop-off (the worst stretch). Most TV ratings sit between 7.5 and 9, so the colour bands are tuned to that range rather than to the full 1 to 10 scale.

What you can do

  • Drag across the strips (or use the slider). The two cards show the episode at the same point in each show's life, with its rating and how it compares to that show's average.
  • Search any show and send it to slot A or B. The 15 built-in shows work offline.
  • Lifespan / Hours: Lifespan stretches both shows to the same width; Hours sizes them by total running time, so you can see the time commitment.
  • Tale of the tape: six head-to-head rounds (average episode, peak run, best season, worst season, consistency and latest season) decide a winner, e.g. “Breaking Bad wins 5–1”. Tap the verdict or Stats for the detail.
  • Share: the link reopens exactly what you're looking at, with the same shows, position and view.
  • Palette (the half-circle button): switches to a colour-blind-safe scale that reads by lightness, not hue.
  • Play Higher or Lower: guess whether the next show (or season) is rated higher or lower. Two modes, Show vs Show and Season vs Season, and it gets harder as your score climbs.

Where the numbers come from

Episode ratings are community ratings from TVmaze. They're great for spotting a show's shape, but they're not gospel: smaller shows have few voters, and some running times are TV slot lengths (Grey's Anatomy is listed at 60 minutes an episode, so Hours mode overstates its time). Long-running soaps and shows with too few ratings aren't drawn rather than drawn badly.

How it's built

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with canvas drawing: no frameworks, no build step and no tracking. It's hosted on GitHub Pages. Your palette choice, game best scores and any shows you search for are remembered on your device only; searches go to TVmaze.
